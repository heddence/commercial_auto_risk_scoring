Commercial auto claim risk

The score estimates the probability that an APD or AL policy line has at least one claim during its supplied one-year period. A company with both coverages can have two scores. The chosen target gives a clear probability between zero and one and supports an agreed policy-review process.

The historical file provides labelled outcomes. After cleaning, 14,990 lines remain. The additional 3,000 records need scores. Claim occurrence does not estimate claim severity or financial loss, so this result alone cannot set a premium. The presentation connects the data findings and modelling choices with the measured results and the steps required for business use.

Two coverages, different claim rates

APD covers damage to the insured business's own vehicles. AL covers third-party bodily injury and property damage. The historical rates differ: about 15.6% of APD lines and 9.1% of AL lines have at least one claim. Across the retained history, 1,853 of 14,990 lines are positive, or 12.36%.

This finding makes coverage an important input and supports checking each coverage separately. A lower AL claim-occurrence rate does not imply lower expected financial loss because severity is outside our target. These are descriptive rates for the supplied historical capture cohorts. All retained policy periods last 365 days, keeping the outcome horizon consistent.

Risk patterns in development history

Outcome-based exploration uses the 2020–2021 development history. APD and AL stay separate in these plots. Larger fleets have a higher probability of at least one claim, which is sensible for this fleet-level target. Prior claims in the same coverage also show a clear association. Higher external scores broadly correspond to higher occurrence, though the AL line is not strictly increasing in every group.

These findings support keeping fleet exposure, prior claims and external risk, and trying coverage-specific history and exposure ratios. They show associations rather than causes. Later permutation checks also highlight coverage, vehicle count and external risk as inputs the fitted model relies on.

Data preparation

Category normalization reveals 75 duplicate copies. Ten rows have expiration before inception. Removing those rows leaves 14,990 usable policy lines. All 3,000 scoring rows remain. Valid active policies also remain, with no July expiration cutoff.

The model uses an allowlist of eighteen raw inputs. Current-claim payments and reporting information reveal the answer and are excluded. We also exclude current-year late payments, identifiers and dates as predictors, and the safety field absent from scoring. Each fitted pipeline learns its own medians, missing-value indicators and category encoding. Evaluation and scoring reuse that fitted preparation. Invalid numeric values become unknown instead of receiving invented corrections. The supplied policy-period labels support retrospective analysis, while their maturity still needs confirmation before deployment.

Historical validation and final scoring

Selection uses two expanding windows in 2021. Fit on earlier records and validate on the next half-year. Every candidate learns its preparation from its own fit partition.

After selection, the chosen configuration fits on 10,023 rows captured in 2020–2021 and evaluates on 4,967 rows captured in 2022. After recording those results, the same configuration refits on all 14,990 usable labels and scores 2023–2024 records. The final model does not generate its own training-set evaluation.

Companies can recur between cohorts, matching the supplied scoring population. A separate company-disjoint check tests the other question of generalization without company overlap. These records include claim outcomes after capture, so this is a retrospective cohort comparison. It does not prove historical input availability. That and complete, mature outcomes require confirmation.

Models and parameter choices

Thirty fixed candidate configurations use the same validation rows. Logistic regression directly models claim occurrence. C controls regularization, with lower values imposing stronger shrinkage. We tested 0.05, 0.2 and 1.0.

Gradient boosting allows nonlinear patterns and interactions. We tested four, eight or sixteen leaves and eighty or one hundred sixty iterations. Other fixed settings include learning rate 0.05, minimum leaf size thirty and L2 regularization twenty. Poisson regression fits claim counts with alpha values 0.01, 0.1 and 1.0, then converts expected counts into occurrence probabilities. That conversion needs an additional Poisson assumption.

We also compared derived inputs, categorical handling, sigmoid probability calibration and premium exclusion. Lowest validation log loss selects the configuration because the intended output is a probability. We preserve the observed prevalence rather than apply class weights or oversampling.

Selected model: regularized logistic regression

The selected model is logistic regression with C equal to 0.05 and seven derived inputs. It has validation log loss 0.357573. The best boosting model reaches 0.357707, so the difference is very small. We keep the selected configuration without treating that small advantage as universal superiority.

The eighteen raw inputs describe coverage, business characteristics, fleet, drivers and prior experience. Derived inputs include same-coverage prior claims, mileage per vehicle, heavy-vehicle share, drivers per vehicle, log prior loss and AL interactions. Preparation handles missing values, standardizes numeric inputs and encodes categories consistently. This whole fitted pipeline is reused for predictions. The chart shows the best configuration in each family and the coverage-rate reference.

Performance on later policies

The 2022 evaluation contains 4,967 lines, with 627 positive outcomes. AUC is 0.675 versus 0.574 for coverage-only scoring. AUC measures ranking: a randomly selected claim line outranks a randomly selected nonclaim line roughly 67.5% of the time. It is not classification accuracy.

Log loss is 0.3597 versus 0.3743, a 3.9% reduction. A company-bootstrap interval for the paired improvement favors the model in this cohort. Average precision is 0.228, against prevalence 0.126. Within coverage, AUC is about 0.660 for APD and 0.646 for AL.

The chart compares predicted and observed rates in six risk groups. Overall mean prediction is 12.15%, versus 12.62% observed. Agreement is broadly reasonable but imperfect. These results support historical ranking and probability assessment, with the timing limitations described earlier.

Prioritizing policy review

Select the 497 highest-scored lines, the top ten percent of the historical evaluation. Of those, 145 have at least one claim. That is a 29.2% occurrence rate, compared with 12.6% overall, or 2.31 times the average rate.

The selected ten percent contains about 23% of all claim-positive lines. This illustrates how the ranking could help allocate review capacity. It does not demonstrate prevented claims or financial savings, and it does not define a universal decision threshold. A pilot needs agreed review rules, operational costs and coverage considerations. Because the score estimates occurrence, claim severity would also matter for financial prioritization.

Scoring population and confidence limits

The final all-history fit produces a probability for every scoring policy ID. Mean predicted risk is 16.2% for APD and 9.5% for AL. These are predictions, not observed rates. The scoring file has no outcome labels, so its performance cannot be measured.

The population has changed. Mean driver age falls from 42.1 to 39.2 years, and stated fleet mileage rises from about 204,000 to 232,000 miles. Rideshare is a new category with sixty lines. Accepting an unseen category does not demonstrate accurate predictions for it. Input monitoring can start immediately. Ranking, calibration and segment performance require mature outcomes, alongside confirmation of what was available at the scoring point.

Production foundations implemented

Four implemented foundations address prototype gaps. Shared preparation and scoring functions validate inputs and apply the same rules. The saved artifact contains fitted preparation and the classifier together, with configuration, fit-cohort and environment metadata.

Output checks verify original row order, unique policy IDs and finite probabilities between zero and one. Reloaded predictions agree with the in-memory model. Saved reports retain validation, evaluation and scoring-quality evidence, including missingness, input shifts and new categories.

These are working prototype foundations. They do not amount to a deployed service with schedules, alerts and incident ownership. The current uploaded notebook also does not record immutable input and code hashes, so stronger run provenance remains part of the roadmap.

Production readiness roadmap

First, agree the scoring point and confirm timestamped inputs and mature claim outcomes. Package a reproducible batch process with automated checks and versioned runs.

Next, shadow-score alongside the current process without changing decisions. Check reliability, shifts and the new rideshare segment. Review mature outcomes before moving to a limited decision-support pilot with agreed rules, access controls, audit trails and rollback.

Finally, assign monitoring and incident owners. Check data quality immediately and evaluate ranking, calibration and coverage performance when labels mature. Retraining and challenger promotion should follow a specified evaluation protocol. Business value needs a defined pilot outcome, rather than an assumption that a better offline metric creates savings.

Learning and AI assistance

The main concepts explored are outcome leakage, separating selection from evaluation and refitting, ranking versus probability quality, and keeping learned preparation in one reusable pipeline. The repaired simple benchmark shows why additional complexity needs evidence.

ChatGPT and Codex assisted with code review and presentation drafting. The case-study instructions permit that assistance and require disclosure. The result is a working historical scoring prototype with explicit limits. A controlled pilot still needs confirmed input timing, mature outcomes and an agreed business decision process.
